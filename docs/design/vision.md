# Vision

**Working title:** Untitled Grand Strategy ("UGS")
**Setting:** The Cold War, starting January 1st, 1950.
**Genre:** Real-time-with-pause grand strategy on an HoI4-scale province map.

## The elevator pitch

Hearts of Iron asks "how do I win the war?" This game asks **"how do I win
the century without ending the world?"** You lead a nation from 1950 onward
through the defining struggle of the 20th century, where total war means
total annihilation and victory is measured in alignment, influence, and
systems that outlast your rival's.

The 1950-01-01 start is deliberate: the player gets six months of "peace" to
learn the game and position themselves before North Korea crosses the 38th
parallel — the first great test of the postwar order, and the game's tutorial
war in all but name.

## The four pillars

Every mechanic must serve at least one pillar; anything serving none gets cut.

1. **Escalation & nuclear brinkmanship** — Tension is a first-class resource.
   Wars are fought under an escalation ceiling both sides manipulate; the
   bomb is the gun on the wall that everyone must point and nobody may fire.
2. **Ideology & influence warfare** — The map is painted by alignment, not
   just occupation. Elections, coups, aid, propaganda, and decolonization
   move borders that armies can't touch.
3. **Intelligence & covert ops** — Information is a fog-of-war weapon and
   deniability is a currency. What your rival *believes* matters as much as
   what is true.
4. **Economic systems competition** — Central planning and market economies
   are mechanically different games, not a slider. Guns vs. butter under
   real budget pressure; the arms race can bankrupt you as surely as losing
   a war can.

## What this game is NOT

- Not a WW3 sandbox where the fun is fighting the big war. The big war is
  the failure state (and still playable — but everyone loses something).
- Not HoI4-with-a-later-date: front-line micro is de-emphasized relative to
  posture, readiness, and proxy commitment.
- Not a strict historical railroad. 1950 is the shared opening position;
  history diverges from the player's first decision.

## Victory

No single "win by conquering X." The record is kept in words at four
dates — 1 January 1955, 1960, 1965 and 1970 — in four terms: the map
(as a delta from 1950 in the nation's own column), the factories,
standing in the chancelleries, and the peace. Every nation is scored
by the same formula; the verdict is WON / HELD / LOST, capped at
COSTLY for anyone who broke the taboo or bled beyond counting, and
there is no verdict for anyone after a general exchange. See
[Era scoring & the verdict](systems/scoring.md).
