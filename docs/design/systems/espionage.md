# Intelligence & Covert Ops

Status: sketch
Pillar: 3

## Core idea

Two distinct products, two distinct games:

1. **Intelligence (knowing things)** — Most of the map is estimates, not
   truth. Enemy army sizes, arsenal counts, industrial output, and intentions
   are shown with confidence bands that your collection assets narrow.
   Rival counterintelligence and deliberate deception widen them or inject
   falsehoods. The UI must render uncertainty honestly: "est. 40–70
   divisions" — and sometimes the estimate is simply wrong.
2. **Covert action (doing things)** — Sabotage, tech theft, election ops,
   coup support, assassinations. Every operation carries an **exposure**
   roll; exposure severity ranges from rumor to smoking gun. Consequences
   route through Tension (escalation doc) and world opinion (influence doc).

## Deniability as a resource

Operations are bought with capability *and* deniability. A blown op spends
deniability globally — your next denial is worth less (the U-2 problem).
Deniability regenerates slowly and can be rebuilt with scapegoats (burn an
agency head) at domestic cost.

## Assets over time

- 1950: agent networks, signals intercepts, defectors.
- Mid-50s: overflights (U-2-alikes) — great collection, catastrophic when
  shot down.
- 60s+: satellites — safe strategic collection, ends the "bomber gap"
  style of being wrong about arsenal counts (a scripted humbling both
  sides should experience before then).

## Open questions

- Agent granularity: named agents (XCOM-flavored) vs. abstract networks per
  country? Leaning abstract networks + rare named defector/mole *events*.
- How does the AI decide what to believe when we feed it deception?
- Player-facing fog: how much wrongness is fun before it feels random?
