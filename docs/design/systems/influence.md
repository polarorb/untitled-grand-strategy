# Ideology & Influence Warfare

Status: sketch
Pillar: 2

## Core idea

Every country has an **alignment position** on a Western↔Eastern axis plus a
**commitment depth** (how locked-in that alignment is: sympathetic → aligned
→ treaty member → satellite). Map painting happens mostly by moving these,
not by occupation.

Influence tools, roughly in order of cost/risk:

- **Aid & trade**: economic aid (Marshall Plan-style), arms sales,
  favorable trade. Slow, safe, expensive.
- **Diplomacy**: treaties, guarantees, bases-for-protection deals.
- **Propaganda & culture**: shifts popular opinion, which drags elected
  governments; matters most in democracies and contested states.
- **Election interference**: cheap, deniable-ish, backfires badly when blown.
- **Coup sponsorship**: fast alignment flip, high failure risk, big Tension
  and world-opinion cost when exposed (feeds the espionage system).

## Decolonization wave

The 1950s–60s produce a stream of new states (India already independent;
Ghana '57, the 1960 African wave, etc.). Each independence event is a
contested opening: both blocs (and a Non-Aligned pull once it exists) compete
for the newborn state. This is the game's renewable strategic frontier —
scripted timing, unscripted outcomes.

## Domestic politics (the mirror)

Your own bloc members have politics too. Satellites can drift (Yugoslavia
already has, 1948), allies can elect unfriendly governments, and heavy-handed
control (crushing an uprising) holds the bloc but costs world opinion and
propaganda ammunition to the other side.

## Open questions

- Is Non-Aligned a passive middle or an active third attractor (Nehru/Tito/
  Nasser as a playable-ish force)?
- Granularity: alignment per-country only, or per-faction within countries
  (army, church, unions) that covert ops can target individually?
